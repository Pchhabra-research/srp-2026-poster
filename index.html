Same Symptoms, Different Stories

Differential relations of suicidal ideation and childhood maltreatment to psychopathology among sexual and gender minority youth

Peehu Chhabra, Alexa M. Rivas, Kaitlin A. Shannon, Angela Karanja, Kaesyn Price, Kathryn Eve Lewandowski, Zachary B. Millman · Division of Psychotic Disorders, McLean Hospital / Harvard Medical School

Key findingsMethodsResultsLimitationsReferencesContact

Key findings

Symptoms that usually travel with suicidal ideation did not do so for sexual and gender minority (SGM) youth in this sample. Childhood maltreatment told more of their story.

1

SGM youth endorsed suicidal ideation at more than twice the rate of non-SGM youth: 44.0% vs 19.5%.

2

In non-SGM youth, suicidal ideation correlated with anxiety and psychosis-risk symptoms (both r = .68). In SGM youth, neither association was present.

3

In SGM youth, childhood maltreatment correlated with anxiety (r = .71) and depression (r = .55). In non-SGM youth, it did not.

4

SGM status significantly moderated three of the four associations tested.

Methods

Participants

N = 66 youth aged 14–29 from a transdiagnostic sample.

21Healthy controls
27Clinical high-risk for psychosis, including 3 meeting SIPS criteria for a psychotic syndrome
18Depressive disorder

Identity groups

SGM (n = 25) was defined as self-identifying as a sexual minority (any orientation other than heterosexual) and/or a gender minority (transgender, nonbinary, or another gender identity). All others formed the non-SGM group (n = 41).

Measures

Depression
Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II), total score
Suicidal ideation
BDI-II item 9; any score above 0 counted as endorsed
Anxiety
Beck Anxiety Inventory (BAI), total score
Psychosis-risk
Prodromal Questionnaire–Brief (PQ-B), total items endorsed
Maltreatment
Childhood Trauma Questionnaire (CTQ), total score

Analyses

SI rates were compared with a chi-square test. Pearson correlations were run separately in each group and compared with Fisher z-tests. Every association that differed by group was then tested as an SGM × predictor interaction in linear regression.

Limitations

  • Small sample, especially within the SGM group (n = 25), limits power and precision.
  • SGM status was unevenly distributed across diagnostic groups (2 of 21 healthy controls vs 14 of 27 CHR and 9 of 18 depression), so group differences may partly reflect clinical status.
  • Cross-sectional design; associations are not causal.
  • Suicidal ideation came from a single self-report item.
